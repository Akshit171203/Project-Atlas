1. The Coffee Plant and Its Origins
Coffee comes from the seeds of flowering shrubs belonging to the genus Coffea, of which two
species dominate commercial production: Coffea arabica and Coffea canephora, commonly known
as arabica and robusta. Arabica accounts for roughly sixty percent of world production and is prized
for its comparatively mild, aromatic flavor, while robusta contains nearly

1.1 Growing Conditions
Coffee thrives in what growers call the 'bean belt,' a band of tropical and subtropical territory that
circles the globe between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn. Ideal arabica cultivation
typically occurs at elevations between 900 and 2,000 meters, where cooler temperatures slow the

2.1 Why Roast Level Changes Taste
Light roasts are removed from the heat before the beans reach the 'second crack,' an audible popping
sound caused by internal gas pressure, and tend to preserve brighter acidity and origin-specific fruit
or floral notes. Dark roasts, carried well past that point, lose much of their original character to the
roasting process itself and instead develop heavier, smokier, and more bitter flavors along with a
noticeable oily sheen on the bean's surface. Contrary to
